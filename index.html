Hybrid workplace planning · Decision brief

Plan for the busy day, not the weekly average.

A seat plan for 2,000 employees across 3 offices, with 3 in-office days each week.

Recommendation

Start with 1,400 shared seats across the portfolio.

That is 0.70 seats per employee: 600 fewer than a one-desk-per-person setup, and 200 above the 1,200-person weekday average implied by a three-days-in-office schedule. Check each location’s busiest days before making space changes.

Scenario Explanations

Three planning scenarios based on the desk-sharing ratio range of 0.6 – 0.75.

✅

Lean case

1,200 Seats

Ratio: 0.6 desks/employee

The smallest footprint in the comparison. It matches the calculated 1,200-person weekday average, so any higher-demand day would need staggered attendance or another capacity plan.

  • ▸ 800 fewer desks than 1:1
  • ▸ No seats above the weekday average
  • ▸ Needs close peak-day monitoring
  • ▸ Depends on reliable scheduling
🎯

Recommended

1,400 Seats

Ratio: 0.7 desks/employee

A middle course for planning: 200 seats above the calculated weekday average and 600 fewer desks than a 1:1 setup. Check the busiest day at each office before adopting it.

  • ▸ 0.70 seats per employee
  • ▸ 30% fewer desks by count
  • ▸ Portfolio-level estimate
  • ▸ Validate with local data
⚠️

Conservative

1,500 Seats

Ratio: 0.75 desks/employee

Keeps more capacity available if attendance is harder to schedule or more variable than expected. It reduces the dedicated-seat count by less.

  • ▸ 500 fewer desks than 1:1
  • ▸ 300 seats above the weekday average
  • ▸ More room for demand variation
  • ▸ Smaller footprint reduction

Weekly Occupancy Trends

See why a weekly average alone is not enough to set a seat count.

The Bimodal Pattern

Attendance can vary through the week. When teams share the same in-office days, Tuesday through Thursday may be busier than Monday or Friday. Use local booking and entry records to see whether that pattern applies here.

For 2,000 employees averaging 3 days/week, the arithmetic gives 1,200 employee-days per weekday. That average does not show whether arrivals cluster on the same day; use booking and entry data to establish the actual peak.

Compare the 0.60–0.75 seat range against observed peaks, then agree a service buffer before changing capacity.

How Scenarios Change the Trend

Lean case (1,200)

Matches average weekday attendance; clustered demand can exceed available seats.

Recommended (1,400)

Adds 200 seats above the average; confirm that this covers the busiest local day.

Conservative (1,500)

Keeps 300 seats above the average, with a smaller reduction from the dedicated-seat baseline.
