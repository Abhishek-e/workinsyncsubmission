Scenario Explanations

Three planning scenarios based on the desk-sharing ratio range of 0.6 – 0.75.

✅

Best Case

1,200 Seats

Ratio: 0.6 desks/employee

Aggressive sharing scenario. High adoption of hoteling policy, excellent booking compliance, low peak-day overlap.

  • ▸ Real estate cost ↓ ~40%
  • ▸ Desk availability ~85% peak
  • ▸ Requires strong enforcement
  • ▸ Mature hybrid culture needed
🎯

Target

1,400 Seats

Ratio: 0.7 desks/employee

Recommended balance. Includes 10–15% buffer above peak demand. Balances cost efficiency with employee experience.

  • ▸ Peak occupancy 85–90%
  • ▸ Cost savings ~30%
  • ▸ Aligns with JLL & CBRE
  • ▸ Best starting point
⚠️

Worst Case

1,500 Seats

Ratio: 0.75 desks/employee

Conservative over-provisioning. Low adoption, resistance to sharing, or extreme peak clustering.

  • ▸ Cost ↓ only ~25%
  • ▸ Desk availability ~100%
  • ▸ Higher utility costs
  • ▸ Risk of "zombie desks"

Weekly Occupancy Trends

Understanding the bimodal pattern that drives desk-sharing ratios.

The Bimodal Pattern

Hybrid attendance is not uniform. It follows a bimodal pattern: Tuesday & Wednesday peak, with Friday dropping sharply. The weekly average (50–60%) hides real peak demand.

For 2,000 employees averaging 3 days/week → weekly avg ~60%. But Tuesday hits 85–95%. Size for average = run out. Size for 100% = over-provision.

Solution: plan for 95th percentile peak + buffer → the 0.6–0.75 ratio range.

How Scenarios Change the Trend

Best Case (1,200)

Peak ~95% Tuesday. Tight but workable with staggered arrivals. Friday drops to ~40%.

Target (1,400)

Peak ~90% Tuesday. Healthy 10% buffer. Friday ~25–30%.

Worst Case (1,500)

Peak ~75% Tuesday. Underutilized most days. Friday ~15% "ghost town".
